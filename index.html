Assessor Website Audit

Three independent AI reviews — ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude — scored gunnisoncounty.org's assessor pages against the same 100‑point civic-service rubric between September 15 and September 28, 2026. Total scores land between 60 and 78. All three flag the same underlying problem from different angles: the site is strong on data tools, weak on keeping deadlines and forms current.

⚠

Published deadlines no longer match state law

SB26‑046 (signed March 26, 2026; effective August 12, 2026) moved the real‑property protest deadline from June 8 to June 1, and aligned the disabled‑veteran exemption deadline to the senior schedule — July 15 regular / August 15 late — instead of the July 1 date still posted. As of the September 28, 2026 review, the Appeals page and the Disabled Veteran exemption page had not been updated. An owner who trusts the county's own June 8 text for the 2027 cycle could file a week too late and lose their right to protest.

Source: Claude review, cross‑checked against leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-046 · gunnisoncounty.org/953/Appeals · gunnisoncounty.org/1069/Disabled-Veteran-Exemption

The research promptSame instructions, sent to ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude — click to read in full ▸

Review the public-facing assessor websites for all 64 Colorado counties. My main goal is to understand where Gunnison County stands statewide and how its website could better serve property owners.

Rubric — same 100 points for every county

CategoryPtsEvaluate
Findability and organization15How easily can a resident find property search, appeals, exemptions, forms, and contact information? Are navigation labels clear?
Property search and public data20Can a user find a parcel by address or account number and view useful values, property details, sales information, and maps? Are the data and their dates explained?
Self-service tasks20Can an owner find and complete or submit common requests, including an appeal, address change, exemption application, property-record correction, or information request?
Explanations and transparency15Does the site explain how property is valued, why a value may change, what the assessor controls, and how value relates to taxes?
Usability across devices10Do menus, searches, forms, and linked portals work clearly on desktop and mobile?
Accessibility10Are core tasks usable with a keyboard, readable text and contrast, meaningful link labels, and accessible forms or documents?
Currency and trust10Are deadlines, rates, notices, contact details, and resources current and clearly dated where appropriate?

Assign letter grades of A (90–100), B (80–89), C (70–79), D (60–69), or F (below 60). Do not award an A if property search is unusable, appeal instructions are materially wrong or missing, or a major accessibility barrier blocks a core task. State the reason if this rule limits a county's grade.

Three tasks, attempted for every county

  1. Find a property record using an address or account/parcel number.
  2. Find the current instructions and deadline for appealing a property value.
  3. Find how to apply for an exemption or correct property information.

Scoring rules

Give every county a numeric score, letter grade, category scores, and brief evidence with links to the pages or tools examined. Distinguish missing, broken, and could not verify. Do not assume a search tool works merely because its link exists. Note when a task moves to a treasurer, GIS, vendor, or other external portal, and assess whether that handoff is understandable. Do not count a service as missing if it is available through a clearly linked external portal.

For each category, explain what earned or lost points using observed evidence. Apply the same scoring standard to every county: award full points when the relevant information or task is easy to find, clear, current, and usable; partial points when it is available but difficult to find, confusing, incomplete, or cumbersome; and zero when it is absent or unusable. Do not treat an unverified feature as missing or award it full credit. Record what you could verify, identify the uncertainty, and explain how it affects the score. Base grades on the property-owner experience, not visual appearance alone.

Gunnison-only depth

Provide detailed improvement recommendations only for Gunnison County. Test Gunnison's full property-owner journey more thoroughly, including finding a record, understanding its value and property details, appealing, finding forms and exemptions, correcting information, and understanding property taxes. For each recommendation, identify the specific problem, the proposed change, why it would help an owner, and its estimated impact and implementation effort. Rank the recommendations by priority. Link to working examples from other Colorado counties when they offer a useful model.

Required order of results

  1. A table of all 64 counties, sorted by score.
  2. Gunnison County's statewide rank and percentile, plus its category scores compared with the statewide median and the highest-scoring counties.
  3. Gunnison's strengths and the specific points where a property owner may get stuck.
  4. Gunnison-only recommendations, ranked by priority.
  5. A short methods section stating the review date, how many of the 64 sites and external portals were actually tested, any limitations, and which findings need manual verification.

Dashboard-ready data

Also provide dashboard-ready data as downloadable CSV files or a single JSON file. Include one record per county with its name, assessor website URL, review date, seven category scores, total score, letter grade, statewide rank, verification status, and brief summary. Provide separate records for Gunnison's recommendations with the issue, proposed change, priority, expected owner impact, estimated effort, and implementation status. Use consistent field names and values. Keep evidence links in the data, use null for scores that cannot be verified, and do not combine multiple counties or recommendations into a single record. Structure the files so they can be used to build a simple interactive dashboard and updated after manual review.

Guardrails against overclaiming

If you cannot examine all 64 counties thoroughly, say so plainly. Do not present a partial sample as a definitive statewide ranking or percentile. Identify which counties were reviewed, label the comparison provisional, and leave unreviewed scores and ranks blank. Do not invent findings or links to fill gaps.

Methods & limitations

  • Three AI systems (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) independently ran the same research prompt: score every Colorado county assessor site on a shared 7-category, 100-point rubric, and complete three property-owner tasks (find a record, find the appeal deadline, find an exemption/correction path).
  • Only Gemini attempted the full 64-county sweep and completed scoring for 24; the other 40 were catalogued by URL only and left unscored, not shown as zero. ChatGPT and Claude scored Gunnison directly and treated statewide ranking as undeterminable given their smaller sample.
  • No review submitted a live appeal, exemption, or correction — all task testing stopped short of actual submission. Mobile-device and formal accessibility (keyboard/screen-reader) testing was not performed by any reviewer; those scores are explicitly marked as estimates above.
  • This dashboard is a synthesis of three AI-generated web reviews, not a legal opinion or a county-verified audit. The SB26-046 deadline claim should be confirmed with county counsel before any page is changed.
